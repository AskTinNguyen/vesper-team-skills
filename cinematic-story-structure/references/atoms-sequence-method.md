# Sequence Method (Screenwriting Down to the Atoms)

Source: *Screenwriting Down to the Atoms* (unnamed author in this edition). The method treats story as built from a Story Spine, divided into story sequences (the "legs" of the protagonist's journey), with each sequence owning its own goal and ending on an escalation or reversal.

## Story Spine to Sequences

- Define the ultimate story goal first, then the consequences of losing it.
- Break the narrative into 8-14 story sequences; each is a self-contained movement with a goal and a turning point.
- A sequence is not accidental; it must connect to the spine and advance toward the goal.
- Group sequences into three acts:
  - Act 1: 2-3 setup sequences ending on the End of First Act Turning Point.
  - Act 2: 5-7 complication sequences (Act 2A builds, Act 2B raises momentum after the midpoint).
  - Act 3: 3-4 resolution sequences driving to the story goal.

## Ending the Second Act

The second act climax can land in one of four ways:

1. The Dark Moment: the hero suffers a terrible defeat that compounds their failure.
2. The False Victory: the protagonist wins on the surface but the win is hollow or reversed.
3. The Key Piece of the Puzzle: the hero gains the missing understanding or asset that unlocks the final plan.
4. The Launch of the Final Plan: the second act ends as the hero commits to and begins the final strategy.

## Scene Construction

A well-structured scene must change something. The four turn types:

1. A Change in Strategy: a character changes approach.
2. A Shift in Power: who holds the advantage changes.
3. A Revelation of New Information: new facts change the reading of the situation.
4. A Situation-Changing Action: an action permanently alters the state of the world.

## Protagonist Requirements

- Humanly relatable: the audience can recognize the person.
- Worthy of the audience's interest: worth watching.
- Worthy of the audience's respect: the choices and sacrifices earn moral standing.
- The antagonist, by contrast, need not be relatable and must generally be the opposite on respect.

## Practical Workflow

1. Write the Story Spine (hero, goal, obstacle, consequences).
2. List 8-14 sequence goals and their turning points.
3. Assign sequences to acts and mark the three act-turning points.
4. Choose how Act 2 ends (Dark Moment / False Victory / Key Piece / Final Plan launch).
5. Draft scenes and verify each turns one of the four ways.
