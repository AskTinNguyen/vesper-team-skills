# McKee Story Principles

Source: Robert McKee, *Story: Substance, Structure, Style, and the Principles of Screenwriting*. This PDF is scanned with no text layer; the principles below are the book's widely established core ideas and should be cross-checked against the actual pages before quoting.

## Core Concepts

- Story value: the quality that can shift between positive and negative charge (love/hate, alive/dead, free/slave). Meaningful change in a story value is what earns audience attention.
- Controlling idea: the story's central truth, stated as a value that changes (e.g., "justice triumphs if the protagonist endures"). Use it to judge every scene.
- Scene: a change in a character's life, measured on a value, caused through conflict. It turns when at least one value goes from positive to negative or vice versa.
- Beat: the smallest unit of story, an exchange of behavior in action/reaction.
- Sequence: a series of scenes around a moderate change that ends on a turn more significant than any scene before.
- Act: a series of sequences around a major value change.
- Story/plot: events arranged in time and causality that lead the protagonist through progressive complications into a deliberate choice, crisis, and resolution.

## Story Design Steps

1. Establish the balance of the protagonist's life that will be upset.
2. Create an inciting incident that breaks that balance and kicks the protagonist toward their goal (or away from it).
3. Raise the stakes through progressive complications, each making it harder and more personal.
4. Force a crisis: the protagonist must make an apparently irretrievable choice between irreconcilable goods/evils.
5. Deliver the climax that resolves the crisis and proves the controlling idea.
6. End on a resolution that shows the new state of the world.

## Conflict and Structure Checks

- Scenes must be driven by conflict between characters (or characters vs. self/society/environment), not by static exposition.
- The gap between expectation and result is where character and dramatic tension live: in every action, the result should deviate from what the character expects.
- Avoid coincidence that solves problems; coincidence may start stories but must be paid for by the protagonist's own choices.
- The climax must be absolute and earned; if the ending can happen without the escalated middle, the structure is broken.
