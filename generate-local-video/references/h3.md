## Generation constraints

- Generate at 24 fps.
- Use 4–15 seconds; the common five-second workflow value is 124 frames.
- Frame counts follow the `17k+5` grid. Let the prepared workflow calculate this from seconds.
- Use dimensions divisible by 32.
- Start near 864×480 for a quick preview. Use approximately 1344×768 for the native-quality 16:9 target.
- R2V supports up to 9 images, 3 videos, 3 audio clips, and 12 media files total. Each reference video/audio clip must be 2–15 seconds; reference audio cannot be the only reference input.

## Prompt structure

Describe subject and scene, chronological shots with timestamps or cut points, camera behavior, motion, visual treatment, and audio in one prompt. Use explicit `<Picture N>`, `<Video N>`, and `<Audio N>` labels in R2V, matching the media input order.

For endpoint images, send the image to the I2V first/last-frame socket; do not describe it as a loose reference. For identity, style, or motion transfer, send it through R2V and state exactly which traits to preserve or transfer.

## Long-form routing

Use `$h3-context-loop` for a planned multi-scene video. It is the verified production workflow: each later scene receives 22 frames (0.917 seconds at 24 fps) of video and generated-audio context, then trims that repeated head before final assembly. It also retains checkpoints and revisions, so a rejected scene can be rerolled or resumed without discarding accepted work.

The proof run used two 124-frame raw scenes. The opening delivered all 124 frames and the continuation delivered 102 after the 22-frame overlap was removed, producing a 226-frame, 9.417-second H.264/AAC output. Treat this as proof of the continuation mechanics, not a promise that any prompt will preserve identity or action without scene-aware writing and review.

Use `$h3-clip-chain` only when an unattended extension can safely continue from one extracted final frame. Its driver does not carry the prior motion or generated-audio tail, so it cannot make a clean dialogue, music, or action handoff reliable. Prefer it for a simple, repetitive visual sequence where speed and length matter more than editability or a controlled scene transition.

For every context-loop continuation, restate the preceding terminal pose, unfinished action, camera path, lighting, environment, ambience, and music at the beginning of the next scene. Hold that inherited state for about two seconds before changing the action, and end the prior scene on a stable transition beat. Review the final two seconds of the accepted scene beside the first two seconds of its continuation before approval.
