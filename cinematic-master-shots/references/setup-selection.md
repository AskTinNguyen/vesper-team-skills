# Setup Selection (Master Shots)

Pick the setup family from the beat's dramatic job, then refine by lens and move. These families come from *Master Shots* Volumes 1-3.

## Fights and chases (Vol 1 Ch 1-2)

- Use long-lens moves to compress distance and sell speed and power (Long Lens Stunt, Long Lens Pan).
- Match or cut motion for conscious "movie fight" clarity; use knock-down and floor work for impact.
- During chases, vary between traveling with the subject and the unseen or momentarily-hidden attacker; add the rare suspense beat so it is not all breathless.
- Sell a chase end with the "almost there" grasp rather than a blur of motion.

## Entrances, exits, reveals (Vol 1 Ch 3)

- Reveal a character through the frame with background reveal, window push, character switch, or direction shift.
- Give each entrance and exit its own punctuation; do not reuse the same reveal at every turn.

## Suspense, searching, shock (Vol 1 Ch 4, 7)

- Let the audience know before the character does (subtle dolly, traces and clues, steps to suspense).
- Withholding is power: shield or delay the attacker, use wide spaces, and let stillness underline fear.
- For shock, misdirect attention first, then move the reveal to a surprising corner of the frame.

## Dramatic shifts and revelations (Vol 1 Ch 5-6)

- Pivot focus with focus-in, pan-and-slide, or working the background; use parallel dolly and eyeline changes to make discoveries land.
- Reveal new information with spatial change (separating, pull-out reveal, mirror door) so the news visibly rearranges the world.

## Directing attention (Vol 1 Ch 8)

- Use object guides, handing-off motion, and color guides to move the eye to the story point.
- Reverse angles and reflections keep the audience oriented while shifting emphasis.

## Cars (Vol 1 Ch 9)

- Coordinate driver and passenger eyelines and the vehicle axis so coverage never flips.
- Front and back seat shooting plus the parked car and car walk give the editor verbal and geographic continuity.

## Dialogue, arguments, intimacy (Vol 1 Ch 10-12, Vol 2)

- Block first, then choose a move that expresses the conversation (Circling Dialogue, Argument in Motion, Staged Glances).
- Vary with power struggles, group conversation, walking and talking, and long distance; avoid the frozen angle-reverse-angle default.
- For intimacy and love and sex scenes, honor consent and emotional honesty; use closeness and facing-up framing to read the beat, not gratuitous angles.

## Advanced moves (Vol 3)

- Blended and reveal moves feel complex from simple setups; short moves heighten speed.
- Match the move to lens and depth; use depth staging and expert framing when the space carries meaning.
- When a complex camera move risks incoherence, return to a disciplined simple move that serves the beat.

## Validation before locking a shot list

- Every setup must be justified by a story turn, not coolness.
- Eyelines, screen direction, and the 180-degree line must stay consistent for the cut.
- The set must be shootable in schedule; prefer coverage that gives the editor choices without redundancy.
