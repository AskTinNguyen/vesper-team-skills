# Weiland Character Arc Map

Source: K.M. Weiland, *Creating Character Arcs*. Represents the arc beat structure from Part 1 (Positive), Part 2 (Flat), and Part 3 (Negative).

## Core Building Blocks (All Arcs)

- The Lie Your Character Believes: the false belief that keeps the protagonist from wholeness.
- The Thing Your Character Wants vs. the Thing Your Character Needs: the Want is the plot goal; the Need is the Truth that will resolve the Lie.
- The Ghost: the past wound that created the Lie.
- The Characteristic Moment: the defining, somewhat extreme action that establishes who the character is.
- The Normal World: the everyday life that reflects the Lie before disruption.
- The Impact Character: the force/person who manifests the Truth and challenges the Lie.

## Positive Change Arc (Part 1)

The protagonist starts one way, learns lessons, and ends in a better place.

1. First Act: establish the Lie, the Want vs. Need, the Ghost, and the Normal World.
2. First Plot Point: force the protagonist out of the Normal World and commit them to the plot (and the Lie's path).
3. First Half of the Second Act: the protagonist chases the Want, the Lie keeps working, and pressure builds.
4. Midpoint: the "moment of truth" glimpse; the character begins to realize the Need, or the Lie is threatened.
5. Second Half of the Second Act: the character resists fully embracing the Truth; intensity climbs through the Third Plot Point.
6. Third Plot Point: a major defeat or revelation that strips away a false support and forces the character to choose between the Lie and the Truth.
7. Third Act: the character begins to act on the Truth.
8. Climax: the character fully chooses the Truth, defeats the Lie (in self or an antagonist embodying it), and earns the Want or its true equivalent.
9. Resolution: the new, healthy normal state is shown.

## Flat Arc (Part 2)

The protagonist already believes in the Truth and stays constant; it is the world/impact character that changes.

- First Act: establish the Truth the protagonist holds and the world that opposes it.
- Second Act: the protagonist pursues goals and is tested; the impact character is challenged by the hero's Truth and begins to change.
- Third Act: the hero's steadfastness brings the external conflict to a head; the climax proves the Truth's viability in the world.
- Resolution: the world, and especially the impact character, is changed by the protagonist's consistency.

## Negative Change Arc (Part 3)

The protagonist ends worse than they started (fall, corruption, disillusionment).

- The protagonist begins attached to a Truth, then progressively betrays it through the Want.
- As the story advances, each act deepens the character's embrace of the Lie and rejection of the Truth.
- The Climax seals the failure: the character "succeeds" at the plot goal but loses their soul, or fails and fully falls.
- Resolution shows the permanent (or tragic) cost of the choice.

## Beat-Placement Checklist

- First Plot Point forces the protagonist to act.
- Midpoint gives an honest look at the Truth (positive arc) or a tempting turn to the Lie (negative arc).
- Third Plot Point is the crisis that forces the deciding choice.
- Climax resolves the inner Lie and the outer conflict in the same stroke.
- Resolution demonstrates the change in the character's everyday behavior, not just their words.
- Rewards and punishments during the arc must teach the lesson at the point where it lands hardest.
