# Arc Diagnostics

Use these prompts to choose, refine, or troubleshoot a character arc (based on the FAQ section of *Creating Character Arcs*).

## Choosing the Arc

- What does the theme demand? If the story proves the protagonist must change to win, use the positive arc. If it proves one person can change a world by staying true, use the flat arc. If it warns about a fall, use the negative arc.
- Does the protagonist's flaw cause the failure in Act 2? If not, the Lie is not doing its job.
- Is there someone who can serve as the impact character manifesting the Truth? If the story transformation is invisible, you likely have no impact character working.

## Building the Inner Life

- How is the Lie holding the character back?
- How is the Lie making the character unhappy or unfulfilled?
- Why does the character believe the Lie (the Ghost)?
- What Truth will the character need to learn?
- What is the plot goal (Want), and how is it an extension of the Thing the Character Wants?
- What must the character lose or sacrifice to earn the Truth?

## Placement Troubleshooting

- Arc is too subtle: make the First Plot Point force a choice the Lie would love and the Truth would make impossible.
- Arc resolves too early: hold the Truth-embrace until the Third Plot Point or Climax.
- Arc feels preachy: show the change through behavior and consequence, never through speeches the character asks the audience to accept.
- Flat arc feels static: the world and impact character must visibly change around the steadfast protagonist; tension comes from the costs of staying true.
- Negative arc feels random: each compromise must be a choice the protagonist actively makes from the Want, with a rising cost and a point of no return.

## Series Check

- For a series spanning many installments, choose whether each installment completes an arc (episodic change) or advances a long arc. Keep a clear "next Lie/Truth" so the character isn't reset or prematurely completed.
- If the protagonist completes their arc early, either change the arc type or introduce a new Want that re-exposes the grounded version of the character.
