# Cinematic Character Arcs

## Overview

Give characters an engine of change, not just a job in the plot. This skill applies Weiland's three arc types (positive, flat, negative) beat by beat and integrates them into the structural placement used by the rest of the cinematic family.

## Workflow

**1. Choose the arc type.**
Decide whether the protagonist needs a Positive Change Arc (character changes for the better), a Flat Arc (character is the Truth-bearer and changes the world around them), or a Negative Change Arc (character falls or is corrupted). Confirm the choice serves theme and genre.

**2. Diagnose the inner life.**
Read `references/weiland-arc-map.md` and answer the core questions: What Lie does the character believe? How does it hold them back and make them unhappy? What Truth will they need to learn? Define the Want (the plot desire) separately from the Need (the Truth that resolves the Lie). The plot goal should be an extension of the Thing the Character Wants.

**3. Root the arc in backstory.**
Define the Ghost: the past wound that caused the character to believe the Lie. Use the Characteristic Moment (the symbolic action that defines them) and the Normal World to show who they are before the change.

**4. Place the arc beats.**
Walk each beat in `references/weiland-arc-map.md` against the story's act structure: First Act, First Plot Point, first/second half of the Second Act, Midpoint, Third Plot Point, Climax, Resolution. Each beat should force the character toward the Truth or away from it.

**5. Add the impact character.**
Identify the impact character who manifests the Truth and challenges the Lie. For a flat-arc protagonist, that impact character changes instead; confirm the story still has transformation somewhere.

**6. Check consequences.**
Verify the character's choices are rewarded or punished in ways that teach the arc's lesson during the story, not just at the end. Confirm series/sequel logic if the arc spans multiple installments.
