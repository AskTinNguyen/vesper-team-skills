# Game Cinematics Production Pipeline (Newman)

Source: Rich Newman, *Cinematic Game Secrets* (PDF). The book runs from a game-industry primer through incorporating film crafts, then building and shipping a cinematic project.

## Part 1 - Production fundamentals

- **Production process:** plan the pipeline around milestones; know who owns each phase.
- **Preproduction:** lock concept, story, and design before expensive production; camera choices and storyboards are decided here.
- **Production:** build levels and assets; treat sound design, motion capture, and voiceover as first-class production work that needs casting and direction.
- **Postproduction:** edit, polish, and integrate the captured material into the game.

## Part 2 - Cinematic crafts

- **Writer:** give the cinematic a scene goal, conflict, and turn; even a gameplay moment needs screenplay logic.
- **Storyboard and concept art:** lock composition, palette, mood, and camera angles before implementation.
- **Cinematography for games:** apply Mascelli's Five C's (camera angles, continuity, cutting, close-ups, and composition) plus the rule of thirds, lighting, staging, and blocking.
- **Producer:** manage risk, preproduction planning, budget, assets, and time; cinematic development is a planned track, not an afterthought.
- **Casting:** cast for voiceover and motion capture deliberately; sides and character breakdowns let auditions target what the cinematic needs.
- **Directing:** run the concept meeting, communicate the vision, direct talent, supervise script and continuity, and direct cut-scenes on set.
- **Sound design:** sound is not an add-on; effective sound work is planned and layered into both gameplay and cinematics.

## Part 3 - Building a cinematic project

- **Getting started:** scope a real project you can finish; assemble the small crew (writer, artist, animator, sound) that the format needs.
- **Game business and legal:** know milestone, revenue, and rights realities before committing.

## Order that catches problems early

1. Fix the story and writer notes.
2. Lock storyboards and concept art (composition and camera).
3. Blueprint cinematography (Five C's, lighting, staging).
4. Plan casting (voice and mo-cap) and directing.
5. Plan the sound design.
6. Produce: capture, direct, and edit.
7. Post: integrate and polish inside the engine.
