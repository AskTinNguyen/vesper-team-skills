# Cinematic Visual Direction

## Overview

Turn a scripted scene into a shootable visual plan. Coverage is the act of shooting the master plus the specific shots the editor needs; blocking and staging determine what the camera can economically capture. The result should give the editor options while honoring screen direction and the emotional intent of the scene.

## Workflow

**1. Analyze the scene before touching the camera.**
Read the scene for story, character, theme, genre, and tone. Answer: who is the main character, why must they act, how do they view the others, where is the climax of the scene, and how does the scene support the theme? See `references/coverage-workflow.md`.

**2. Block the actors and map the space.**
Establish blocking from the script, the location, and the character relationships. Draw a floor plan with camera symbols before building the shot list. Confirm the dominant axis and the 180-degree line so coverage stays consistent. Staging drives coverage more than the reverse.

**3. Choose the coverage architecture.**
Decide the master approach (continuity master vs. shot-by-shot coverage vs. a moving master) and the coverage package: master, two-shot, overs, singles, inserts. Order shots to serve the scene's turning points and the editing plan.

**4. Build the shot list.**
Use the template in `references/shot-list-template.md`: shot number, size, lens, camera height, move, eyeline, action, and cutting order. Number by shot flow, not shoot order; place a "part 1/2/3" marker on pages that continue across coverage sections.

**5. Storyboard and pre-visualize.**
Translate the shot list into storyboard panels and floor-plan diagrams in the same order as the shot list (cutting order). Panels must be consistent with the 180-degree line. Use the staging notation in `references/staging-notation.md` for camera/actor moves.

**6. Stress-test for schedule.**
Review the plan as both director and production manager: is every setup necessary, can the day be shot in time, and does the coverage provide fallback options for the edit? Cut redundant masters and overlong coverage.

**7. Allow room to adjust on set.**
The plan is a working document. Re-block with actors, let new discoveries change the shots, and rework the shot list when the reality of the location or performance demands it.

## Camera Language (Katz)

- Continuity style is the default grammar (99% of what audiences have watched); learn it before breaking it.
- Spatial connections (composition) and temporal connections (editing) are the two halves of visual narrative.
- Moving-camera work (pan, crane, tracking) should be motivated by what the scene needs, not decoration. See Katz's Live-Action camera chapters for staging the moving camera.
- Depth of the frame: use foreground, midground, and background staging to create spatial storytelling.
