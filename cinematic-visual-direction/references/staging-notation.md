# Staging and Diagram Notation (Katz)

Source: Steven D. Katz, *Cinematic Motion* and *Shot by Shot*. Follow this notation so storyboards and floor-plan diagrams are unambiguous.

## Camera Symbols

- Outline camera = the starting position of a move.
- Solid/black camera = the final position of a move.
- Camera with a curved arrow through it = a pan (lateral rotation).
- Multiple outline cameras = intermediate stages of a complex move.
- A dotted path with camera symbols = the camera's travel through the set.

## Actor Symbols

- Outline figure = actor's starting position.
- Solid/black figure = actor's final position.
- Broken line connecting outline and solid figures = actor's path/direction of movement.
- Arrow crossing the frame border = subject enters or exits the frame.
- Arrow beginning at the subject = the path the subject takes through the frame.

## Storyboard Cards

- Each card carries a shot number; a single continuing move uses the same number with letter suffixes (1a, 1b, 1c) and an ellipsis to indicate the shot continues.
- A change in shot number begins with "Cut To:" on the new card.
- Include dialogue underneath the description. Example:

```
Description appears here.
ANN: Dialogue appears here.
```

## Spatial Consistency

- Keep the 180-degree axis in mind on every card; arrows and eyelines must not flip the audience's sense of space without an explicit re-establishment.
- Use foreground/midground/background staging (depth of frame) to layer meaning, especially in single-camera masters.
- When using wide aspect ratio (e.g., 1.85:1), preserve the frame edges that the final format will actually present.

## Workflow Note

Two-page spreads pair the on-screen storyboard card with the floor-plan diagram of camera path and choreography. Always generate both together so the "what the audience sees" and the "how it was staged" are verifiable against each other.
