# QA Checklist

## Must pass

- 16:9 horizontal format.
- Clean white background.
- Xiaohei is present.
- Xiaohei performs the core action, not decoration.
- The composition is not copied from old examples; it creates a fresh metaphor for the current article.
- The image is weird, creative, and interesting.
- Clean and airy; main subject does not exceed about 60% of the canvas.
- One image expresses one core structure.
- Chinese annotations are few, short, and readable.
- Orange is used only for main paths or arrows.
- Red is used only for emphasis, problems, reminders, or outcomes.
- Blue is used only for supplementary notes, feedback, or system state.

## Failure signals

Regenerate or edit if any of these appear:

- Top-left title such as “Common Pitfalls”, “Workflow”, “System Architecture”, or “Roadmap”.
- Xiaohei looks like a mascot, meme, or cute cartoon.
- The image looks like a PPT slide, course slide, or formal flowchart.
- Too many elements, arrows, or nodes.
- Text becomes long explanatory paragraphs.
- Background has paper texture, shadow, gradient, beige tone, or noise.
- Real UI screenshots or tech-interface visuals.
- Severe Chinese typos or unreadable labels.
- The image is too rigid and lacks an absurd metaphor.
- Composition is too similar to old cases in `assets/examples/`.

## Iteration methods

- Too ordinary: make Xiaohei the action subject and add a strange but valid metaphor.
- Too complex: delete nodes; keep one action and 3-5 short annotations.
- Too cute: emphasize deadpan, blank serious expression, not cute, not mascot.
- Too PPT-like: remove titles, borders, tidy grids, and excessive arrows; turn it into a hand-drawn scene.
- Too similar to old examples: keep the core meaning, replace the main object and Xiaohei action.
- Text is wrong: prefer local editing; if too many labels are wrong, regenerate with fewer annotations.

## Delivery judgment

A high-quality image should make the reader first think “a bit weird”, then understand the structure within about one second.

If the first impression is a tutorial slide rather than a strange product sketch on white paper, it fails.
