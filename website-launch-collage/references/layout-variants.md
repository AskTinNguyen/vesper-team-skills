# Layout variants

Choose one primary structure and adapt it to the product's visual language.

## Editorial desk

Use a warm, cool, or neutral paper-like surface. Place the overview screen large
and left/back, the detail screen center/front, the feature screen lower-right,
and the message panel in the open area. Add restrained rules, labels, grain, and
soft physical shadows. Best for documentation-heavy products and design tools.

## Dark product wall

Use a matte charcoal or near-black ground. Float two or three bright screenshot
panels with narrow frames and a single high-contrast message card. Keep the
accent color sparse. Best for developer tools, infrastructure, games, and
technical launches.

## Structured evidence board

Use an asymmetric grid with one dominant screenshot, two smaller evidence
tiles, a compact metric/proof strip, and a message panel aligned to the grid.
Use thin lines and deliberate numbering rather than decorative clutter. Best for
dashboards, analytics, workflow products, and operational milestones.

## Device/tabletop

Frame screenshots as cards, tablets, or tabletop artifacts with modest depth.
Do not add fake hardware specifications or imply a native device app when the
product is a website. Best when the product needs a more tangible, approachable
internal-demo feel.

## Composition checks

- The overview is the first visual anchor.
- The detail screen explains what changed or why it matters.
- The feature screen supplies concrete proof.
- The message panel contains the communication, not the product UI.
- At thumbnail size, the image still reads as one product story rather than a
  pile of screenshots.
