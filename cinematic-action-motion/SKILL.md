# Cinematic Action Motion

## Overview

Design action that works the audience up. Hong Kong action cinema treats the action scene as "motion emotion": the staging, editing, and rhythm must make danger and skill vivid, legible, and elating. The central craft is choosing how much to show honestly in one shot versus how much to assemble across cuts, and doing it in a way the audience can always read.

## Workflow

**1. Ground the fight in character and story.**
Decide what the action means before designing it: who is the fighter, what fighting logic fits them, and how the beat changes the balance of power. Read `references/fight-style-language.md` and match the style to the character, not the other way around.

**2. Choose the staging approach.**
Decide between the long-take approach (reveal genuine skill and exact choreography) and constructive editing (assemble the action from separate shots to fake or extend feats). Read `references/action-aesthetics.md` for when each is honest, economical, or spectacular.

**3. Engineer legibility.**
Make every shot readable: clear spacing and positions, consistent screen direction and eyelines, and, for constructed sequences, a mental map the audience can assemble. If the geography is ever unclear, the violence reads as chaos, not skill.

**4. Control motion and speed.**
Use speed deliberately: slow motion to smooth or to signal quality, speeded motion and reverse motion for stunts and trick effects, and frame-rate control when you want precise rhythm. Every speed choice should serve the repertoire of the beat.

**5. Build rhythm with shot length.**
Cut on the action's turns and beats. Brevity sells force: give the decisive blow the shortest shot and let the defeated character's fall or spin read longer. Read `references/action-aesthetics.md` for the King Boxer breakdown as the model.

**6. Layer the payoff and escalation.**
Put visible risk and escalating stakes on screen, then resolve on an image that pays off the opening figure. Show the danger (a stunt, a near-miss, the damage) rather than only the blood.

**7. Fit the genre formula.**
Reference `references/genre-formula.md` when the scene must satisfy genre expectations and travel to a broad audience. In popular action cinema, innovation happens inside a familiar framework, and multi-genre films blend action with comedy, romance, or plot to broaden appeal.

**8. Review against the audience's read.**
Trace the sequence from a cold viewer's perspective: can they follow who is fighting whom, where the space is, what lands, and who wins? Fix any shot that takes the scene out of the cuttable, readable frame.

## Camera Language

- Watertight continuity: legible composition plus continuity editing is what lets grisly or spectacular action travel.
- Constructive editing builds an impossible event from plausible fragments; denying an establishing shot forces the audience to assemble the action mentally.
- Long takes and panning cameras display real choreography, especially when the space can be rehearsed in a studio.
- Motion is emotion: shot length and camera speed are how the audience feels the force of the blow.
